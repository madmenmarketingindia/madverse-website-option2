Growth Thinking

The case for fewer,
better-connected
growth decisions.

More activity creates motion. Growth comes from concentrating effort on choices that connect, learn and make the next move stronger.

By
Madverse Editorial
Read
05 minutes
Topic
Growth

Share by email ↗

Growth rarely stalls because a business has too few ideas. It stalls because too many disconnected choices compete for the same attention.

01 · The activity tax

Every disconnected initiative makes the whole system work harder.

Activity is reassuring because it is visible. Campaigns launch, channels fill, dashboards move and teams remain busy. When growth pressure rises, adding another initiative can feel more responsible than choosing which existing effort should stop. The organisation accumulates motion even when the direction remains unclear.

Disconnection creates a tax that rarely appears in a campaign budget. Each project needs its own explanation, audience logic, creative language and measurement story. Teams repeatedly rebuild context. Customers receive fragments that may each be competent but do not reinforce one another. Learning remains trapped inside the initiative that produced it.

The cost becomes greater as the organisation scales. More channels and specialists create more possible combinations, but without a shared growth direction they also create more opportunities for divergence. Coordination expands while coherence declines. Senior people spend time resolving collisions that a clearer set of choices could have prevented.

Fewer decisions do not mean lower ambition. They mean deciding what must compound. A focused promise, priority audience, behaviour to influence and evidence of progress can organise many different activities without making them identical. The reduction happens at the level of strategic choice, not creative possibility.

02 · Connection creates leverage

Make each result useful beyond the moment that produced it.

Connected growth begins with one direction that brand, experience and performance can interpret through their own disciplines. Brand builds memory and meaning. Experience turns the promise into something people can use or feel. Performance reveals which messages, audiences and moments create movement. Each part has a different role, but all of them improve the same system.

This connection changes how teams define success. A performance result is not only an efficiency score; it is evidence that may sharpen the proposition. A service friction is not only an operational issue; it may reveal where the brand promise is weak. A campaign is not finished when media ends if its distinctive assets and learning can strengthen what follows.

Connection also improves prioritisation. A proposed initiative should be able to explain which part of the growth system it strengthens and what new intelligence it will create. If it cannot, its value may be temporary even if its immediate output appears attractive. This makes stopping work a strategic act rather than a sign of reduced energy.

A connected system still needs experiments. The difference is that experiments begin from shared context and return learning to it. Teams can test different expressions, journeys or channel roles without creating a new strategy for every test. Variation becomes a way to learn, not a reason to fragment.

The discipline is to maintain a rhythm: choose, act, observe, connect the learning and choose again. Momentum comes from the quality of that loop. When information travels between disciplines, the organisation does not merely produce more. It becomes better at deciding what deserves production.

“The most valuable growth decision is often the one that makes several other decisions unnecessary.”

03 · A decision filter

Choose work that strengthens more than its own result.

  1. 01

    Does this decision strengthen the shared growth direction or introduce a competing one?

  2. 02

    Will the work build an asset, capability or piece of learning that remains useful afterward?

  3. 03

    Are the right disciplines connected early enough to shape the answer together?

  4. 04

    What existing activity should stop, combine or change if this initiative becomes a priority?

  5. 05

    How will evidence return to the system and improve the next consequential choice?
